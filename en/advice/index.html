Six articles and who they help

  • Sewer smell in a flat: a dried-out trap in an unused shower, a loose seal at the toilet, a washing machine drain.
  • No hot water in the flat: a heating network test, a problem in the building, or your own mixer tap.
  • Bleeding radiators: the bleed valve, the key, a rag, and pressure in a private house.
  • Bath or shower: what to choose in the narrow bathroom of a series block.
  • Combining the bathroom and toilet: what to ask the building manager and why the riser must stay accessible.
  • Hot water smells: the anode, bacteria and descaling.

The articles are written for both types of building. In a series-block flat, part of the answer is often with the building manager or Rīgas siltums, while in a private house everything is in the owner’s hands, starting with the boiler and the water heater.

If the check solves nothing, you will find the jobs and prices on the plumbing services page.

When it is better to put the check off

The articles are meant for a calm moment. If water is running onto the floor or to a neighbour, first close the valve in the flat or on the incoming supply pipe and switch off the water heater, then read the emergency plumber page.

In a multi-flat building, do not touch what is shared: the riser in the toilet duct, the valves in the basement and the radiator valves on the shared heating riser. That is the building manager’s responsibility, and the articles say when to call them.
