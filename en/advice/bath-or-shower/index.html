Bath or shower - what each asks of the bathroom

Bath or shower in an Iļģuciems flat is not only a matter of taste: each asks something different of the floor, the walls, the drain and the hot water. A bath needs firm support and access to the trap, a low shower needs a drain that takes water almost at floor level. If there are children or an older person at home, it also matters how high you have to step and what happens to the water temperature.

Five things you can check yourself

  1. Measure how high you have to step. The manufacturer Ravak gives the top edge of its Rosa acrylic bath as 610 mm above the floor, and the fitting height of its Gigant Pro cast-marble shower tray as 3 cm. If someone in the family has trouble stepping over a bath rim, these two numbers decide the choice.
  2. See how the bath stands. On feet or bricked in. Ravak states that an acrylic bath must never be built in rigidly, because it is carried by a reinforced base with adjustable feet. Taking out an old bath that is bricked in means breaking the brickwork.
  3. Find the access to the trap. For a built-in bath, Ravak requires an opening to be left in one wall for access to the trap and the supply pipes. If your bath has none, the trap can be cleaned or replaced only by removing the finish.
  4. Find out where the hot water comes from. From the building’s hot water riser or from your own water heater. This matters for a thermostatic mixer, see below.
  5. Assess the wall and floor finish. A Ravak bath is installed only in a room that is fully finished and tiled. If the tiles still have to be changed, the order is: tiles, then the bath.

A steel bath and an acrylic bath are not the same job

The manufacturer Kaldewei requires a drain with a double-lip seal for a steel enamel bath and shower tray. The bath is loaded before the flexible joint is made, and the load is left until it has dried completely. Mortar or adhesive must not touch the bath’s lower edge directly; a bath sealing tape is advised for this.

For an acrylic bath Ravak leaves a gap of 2–3 mm between the bath and the tiles, which is then filled with silicone. Ravak also advises filling the bath first with cold water and only then with increasingly warmer water.

A thermostatic mixer for children and older people

The manufacturer Grohe gives its thermostatic mixer a safety stop at 38 °C, so the temperature handle stops at this mark. This helps both for a bath and for a shower when a child washes, or a person who reacts slowly to hot water.

Grohe also states requirements: the hot water at the supply must be at least 2 °C warmer than the desired temperature, the minimum flow is 5 l/⁠min, and above 5 bar of static pressure a pressure reducer is needed. A thermostatic mixer does not work with unpressurised (open-vented) water heaters, and with an instantaneous heater only if its power is at least 18 kW.

The brick walls stay, what changes is what is between them

The first standard blocks of Iļģuciems, series 303, were built with load-bearing brick walls, and later the district was built from five-storey large-panel sections. In both cases the bathroom walls are not usually moved, so the space for a shower comes in place of the bath.

LBN 200-21, clause 134: in flats, sanitary rooms are designed so that they can, if needed, be adapted for people with mobility impairments without affecting the load-bearing structures. Checked 16.11.2026.

A tray with no threshold and a low fitting height is one way to make a bathroom more convenient without changing the walls. Whether your drain allows it can be seen only on site.

When the choice has to become a job

Placing and connecting a new bath is from €60, removing the old bath is separate. Assembling and connecting a shower cabin is from €125 per set. For the job “shower instead of a bath” there is no single price; it is set on site after these factors:

  • the old bath: it has to be taken out and carried away;
  • what goes in its place: a shower cabin, a tray or a shower area with a drain;
  • the drain: whether its height or position has to change to get a fall;
  • the supply pipes: whether the water pipes and the mixer tap need moving;
  • the floor height and how easy it is to reach the riser.

All are labour prices; materials are extra. The jobs are described on the pages bath replacement and shower cabin installation. On the call you talk through the job and the price.

Questions residents ask

Can a steel enamel bath be cleaned with vinegar?

Kaldewei advises removing limescale from the enamel with a mixture of lukewarm water and vinegar 1:1, not with concentrated vinegar. Abrasive products, steel wool, strong acids and alkalis are not used.

We have a water heater. Does a thermostatic shower mixer work?

Grohe states that thermostatic mixers cannot be used with unpressurised (open-vented) heaters. If the water heater is connected under pressure, compare the other requirements: hot water at least 2 °C warmer than the desired temperature and a flow of at least 5 l/⁠min. More about water heaters on the page water heaters.

Can an acrylic bath be set in concrete so that it does not move?

No. Ravak states that an acrylic bath must not be built in rigidly: it is carried by a base with adjustable feet, and a 2–3 mm silicone joint is left between the bath and the tiles.
