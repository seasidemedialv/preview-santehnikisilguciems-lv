Toilet installation - what to decide before you buy

Toilet installation in Iļģuciems and the surrounding area is largely decided already in the shop: the new toilet must fit your toilet room, work at the building’s water pressure and connect to the existing drain.

A toilet with a cistern is flat equipment, so you decide on replacing it yourselves, but the riser behind it serves the whole building. The work is from €50 per toilet, the toilet and materials extra.

One toilet job: what is in and what is out

The price from €50 includes:

  • removing the old toilet;
  • fixing the new toilet to the floor or to the frame;
  • connecting to the water supply and the drains.

Not included and charged separately:

  • the toilet, cistern, collar, supply hose and other materials;
  • a new drain pipe from the toilet to the riser: from €30 per connection point;
  • each new water or drain connection point, for example for a bidet: from €35 per point;
  • work on the shared riser, which the building manager decides.

A toilet room 0.8 m wide: will the new toilet fit

The building code LBN 200-21 requires a new toilet room to be at least 0.8 m wide and 1.4 m long, and at least 2.2 m high to the ceiling or the services box. Iļģuciems mostly has brick blocks of series 316/318 and five-storey panel blocks of series 464, where the toilet is often separate and small.

If the room is close to these dimensions, every centimetre of toilet length is felt between the edge of the toilet and the door. Before buying, compare the length of the new toilet with the old one and check that the door will still open after the replacement.

LBN 200-21 clause 118 (toilet width and length) and sub-clause 7.4 (height in sanitary rooms). Checked 16.11.2026.

The pressure the new set is designed for

The mechanism of a close-coupled toilet cistern has its own pressure window, and it differs by model. The manufacturer Cersanit states a water supply pressure of 0.5 to 6 bar for its ceramics and close-coupled toilet mechanisms. The manufacturer Ideal Standard states a fill valve range of 0.2–8.0 bar for one of its close-coupled toilets, and above 3.5 bar the flow restrictor supplied in the set is fitted in the inlet.

On the lower floors of a nine-storey series 602 block or a 12-storey block the pressure is usually higher than on the upper ones. If you do not know your pressure, it can be measured with a pressure gauge at a tap, and then compared with the figure on the new toilet’s packaging or in its instructions.

A new set: what to do before the cistern fills

  1. Inspect the ceramic before fitting Cersanit states that the product must be checked before installation, and if a defect is found, it must not be installed. So the ceramic is inspected before it is screwed down.
  2. Set the flush volume while the valve is still outside For one model Ideal Standard sets the flush valve to 6/4 litres (full and partial flush) according to a table, before the valve is put in the cistern.
  3. Fit a restrictor at high pressure If the pressure is above the manufacturer’s limit, for example above 3.5 bar for the Ideal Standard valve mentioned, the flow restrictor from the set is fitted in the inlet.
  4. Tighten the nuts with measure Ideal Standard warns that nuts tightened too hard can crack the cistern or the toilet. Even force from both sides matters more than strength.
  5. No mortar or sand in the cistern Cersanit states that mortar, lime and sand in the cistern interfere with the mechanisms. So the cistern is connected when the dusty work in the room is finished.
  6. Connect the outlet and check The outlet is joined to the socket through the collar, the valve is opened and after several flushes the joints are inspected from below.

Tape measure and phone: data for choosing the toilet

  • the width and length of the toilet room, the direction the door opens;
  • the distance from the wall to the centre of the socket, and whether the old outlet goes into the floor or the wall;
  • the building type and floor, for example series 464, 3rd floor;
  • the model of the new toilet or a link to it;
  • whether a bidet or a bidet shower is needed next to it;
  • a photo from the side showing the socket, the valve and the riser box.

Bidet installation in a series-block toilet room

A bidet needs cold and hot water and its own drain, while a toilet needs only a cold water supply. So new connection points usually have to be built next to the toilet. In a narrow toilet room a bidet often does not fit, and then a bidet shower is chosen: a small shower head with its own mixer tap by the toilet.

Installing a wall-hung toilet on a frame is a different job from a floor-standing toilet, because the frame is fitted first and the toilet only after the wall finish. What it costs in your toilet room, you talk through on the call.

Who decides: the toilet is yours, the riser is the building’s

The Apartment Ownership Law counts the toilet fittings and the services up to the shared risers as part of the flat’s separate property. In your own flat, work can be done without agreement with the other owners, as far as it does not touch common property or other flats.

In practice this means: replacing the toilet is usually your own decision. If the riser socket has to be touched or the riser shut off, ask your building manager, unless the building’s documents say otherwise. If the toilet is whole and only leaks, toilet repair is enough.

Example: a toilet and a bidet in one toilet room

Replacing a toilet is from €50. If a bidet is also needed, with two new water supplies and one new drain, that is three connection points at €35 each: €50 + 3 × €35 = at least €155 labour only; materials are extra. Whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work.

Questions residents ask

Do I have to tell the building manager after replacing a toilet?

If you replace only the toilet and do not touch the riser, the Apartment Ownership Law allows work in your own flat without the other owners' agreement. If the riser has to be shut off or the socket replaced, that is usually the building manager’s decision.

I live on the ground floor of a series 602 block. Will the high pressure damage the new cistern valve?

Compare your pressure with the manufacturer’s limits. Cersanit states 0.5–6 bar, and one Ideal Standard model requires a flow restrictor above 3.5 bar, which is in the set. If the pressure is not known, it is measured at a tap before the work.

Which toilet fits if the toilet room is only a little wider than 0.8 m?

Choose a shorter toilet and compare its length with the old one. In the form give the toilet room dimensions and add a photo from the side, and then on the call you can be told whether the chosen model will fit up to the door.
