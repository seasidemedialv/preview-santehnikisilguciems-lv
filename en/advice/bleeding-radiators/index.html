Bleeding radiators - the key, the valve and the order

Bleeding a radiator starts with the right tool: a valve with a square stem needs a bleed key, a valve with a slot takes a flat screwdriver. Open the valve slowly, only a quarter to half a turn, let the air hiss out and close it when water appears. In Iļģuciems and nearby you can do this yourself in a series flat and in a private house, but in a private house the pressure has to be topped up afterwards.

What to look at on the radiator before you take the key

  1. The valve’s position The bleed valve is in the radiator’s upper corner. If the radiator is cool right at the top, there is most likely air there.
  2. The valve head A square stem means a bleed key, a slot means a flat screwdriver. Get the tool in advance, not at the moment the radiator is already cold.
  3. Paint on the valve If the valve is painted over, carefully clean the paint from around the head. If it still does not turn, do not force it: a broken valve is replaced by a plumber.
  4. The thermostatic head’s number If the thermostatic head is turned to zero or to the frost symbol, the radiator can be cold without any air at all. IMI Heimeier states that for heads with a zero position the valve opens only at about 0 °C.
  5. The pressure gauge in a private house Write down what the boiler’s pressure gauge shows. After bleeding you will have something to compare with.

Bleeding as Stelrad recommends

  1. A cloth under the valve Hold a folded cloth or a small container under the valve: after the air comes water, and it can be hot and dark.
  2. A quarter to half a turn Turn anticlockwise slowly. Stelrad advises never opening the valve fully.
  3. Close when water comes When a steady trickle replaces the hiss, close the valve without extra force.

In a two-storey private house Stelrad advises starting with the radiators on the lower floor and only then going upstairs.

Why air appears on the upper floor first

The Grīvas iela estate, whose construction began in 1958–1960, has six-storey blocks with artists' studios in the attic. The highest radiators there are under the roof, and air in the system rises straight to the highest point. The same happens in Iļģuciems’s nine-storey series 602 blocks and in the 12-storey buildings of the Czech design, where the riser is long.

Air often appears in autumn, when the riser is refilled after the summer work. The flat’s valve lets out only the air that is in your radiator. If several radiators on one riser are cold, or the pipe that comes to the radiator already is, report it to your building manager; the check of valves and supply pipes is described on the page radiator repair.

The radiator is cool but there is no air

If the valve hisses only for a moment or water comes at once, air is not the fault. Two places to check:

  • The thermostatic head: the setting range of the IMI Heimeier K head is 6–28 °C with a frost-protection function. At the frost symbol the radiator heats only enough to keep the room from freezing.
  • The valve setting: under the head a Danfoss RA-N valve can have a step from 1 to 7 set, and a lower number limits the flow. The manufacturer states that in one-pipe systems the setting must be N, fully open.

In an apartment building the valve and the radiator are part of the building’s system, so do not change the setting yourself: ask your building manager.

In a private house after bleeding: topping up

Together with the air, water also leaves the closed loop, so the pressure gauge shows less than before the work. The filling tap is usually at the boiler: it is either a built-in valve or a short hose between the water supply and the heating loop. Open it slowly and close it when the reading reaches the required value; disconnect the hose afterwards. The expansion vessel’s pre-charge pressure is usually written on the vessel’s label.

Flamco (an expansion vessel manufacturer): after the system has been heated up, when the water has cooled to about 50 °C, the system is topped up to 0.3 bar above the expansion vessel’s pre-charge pressure. Checked 16.11.2026.

When bleeding should be left to a plumber

Stelrad writes: if radiators have to be bled often, there is another cause, and a specialist should be found. In a private house air often comes with new water that is added because something leaks somewhere; if the lower part of a radiator stays cold, heating system flushing helps. In an apartment building, repeated air on an upper floor is a reason to write to the building manager.

The plumber’s call-out and inspection is from €40. There is no single price for releasing air from radiators. It depends on how many radiators have to be bled, whether water has to be added and the pressure raised in a private house system, and whether the air returns and the source has to be found.

Questions residents ask

The valve is painted over and the key does not fit. What should I do?

First look at the head: a square needs a bleed key, a slot a flat screwdriver. Carefully clean the paint from around the head. If the valve does not turn even then, do not force it and book a plumber.

In a series flat on the top floor the radiator is full of air again after every summer. Why?

In summer the riser is often drained for repairs and refilled in autumn. The air from it collects at the highest point, in the top-floor radiator. Bleeding once is normal; if it repeats all winter, report it to your building manager.
