The two are fitted differently: an acrylic bath stands on its own feet and must not be built in, and a steel bath is separated from the mortar by a tape. Placing and connecting the new bath is from €60, and taking away the old bath is a separate job.

What is in the line from €60 per bath

  • Included: placing the new bath.
  • Included: a trap with an overflow.
  • Included: connection to the existing drain.
  • Not included: disconnecting, carrying out and taking away the old bath.
  • Not included: replacing the mixer tap, from €25 per unit.
  • Not included: the drain pipe to the riser, from €30 per connection point.
  • Not included: the bath, the panel and the tiles.

If more work turns up during the job (for example a damaged drain inlet under the old bath), the Consumer Rights Protection Centre (PTAC) advises drawing up a record for the additional work with an estimate approved by the customer.

Old one out, new one in: the order

  1. Panel and trap If the old bath sits inside a tiled panel, the panel is knocked down, then the trap is disconnected from the drain inlet.
  2. Carrying out down the stairs The Iļģuciems residential district was planned with five-storey large-panel sections, and such blocks usually have no lift. A heavy bath is carried down the stairs, so this work has its own price.
  3. Place for the new bath For an acrylic bath the tiles must already be done. For a steel bath, a support is prepared so that mortar is not put directly against the enamel.
  4. Placing and the drain The bath is levelled on its feet, and the trap with the overflow and the drain are connected.
  5. Check before the panel Kaldewei requires the drain to be checked after connection and before the final finish, while the trap can still be reached.
  6. The joint The joint along the wall is done last: for an acrylic bath in the gap, for a steel bath with the bath loaded.

Facts about the bath and bathroom for the request

  • The material, maker, model and dimensions of the new bath, and whether it has already been bought.
  • The material of the old bath, and a panel around it if there is one.
  • Whether the tiles in the bathroom are already done.
  • The floor, the lift and the turns of the staircase.
  • Whether to replace the mixer tap too and where it is: in the wall or on the edge of the bath.
  • Photographs: the bath along its whole length, the trap, the mixer tap and the bathroom door.

A shower in place of the bath

A shower in place of a bath has no single figure in the price list, because the work depends on what is under the bath and behind the wall. The price depends on these jobs:

  • taking the old bath out and carrying it away;
  • what replaces it: a cabin, a tray, or a tiled shower area with a floor drain;
  • whether the drain has to change height or place to get a slope;
  • whether the water supplies and the mixer tap need moving;
  • the floor height and access to the riser.

The cabin work is described on the page shower cabin installation, and the choice between the two on the page bath or shower.

Questions residents ask

Can an acrylic bath be built into brickwork so that it does not rock?

Ravak states that an acrylic bath is never built in rigidly: the load is carried by the reinforced base with adjustable feet. A panel can be put in front, but the bath must stand on its feet.

How do I fill a new acrylic bath the first time?

Ravak advises running cold water in first and then gradually warmer water.

What removes limescale from the enamel of a steel bath?

Kaldewei advises lukewarm water with vinegar in a 1:1 ratio, not concentrated vinegar. Abrasive products, steel wool, strong acids and alkalis are not used.

Does a bath replacement need a written contract?

PTAC explains that a contract can also be concluded orally, but a written one makes it easier to prove in a dispute. Even with an oral agreement, the contract and the payment can be proved, for example, with messages in correspondence.
