Sewer smell in a flat - when it appears and what it means

A sewer smell in a flat on Riga’s west side usually means that somewhere between the room and the pipes there is no longer any water or a tight seal. The time the smell appears tells you where to look: after an absence it is a dried-out trap, only at the toilet a loose connection, after washing the washing machine’s drain hose. Most of the checks you can do yourself with a bucket of water.

Checks by when it smells

  1. After a holiday or a long absence The manufacturer (Oatey) states that in normal household conditions a trap dries out completely in 2–4 weeks. Pour water into every drain: sinks, bath, shower and floor drain. If the smell goes away afterwards, the cause was an empty trap and no repair is needed.
  2. Only in one room, with a rarely used shower or drain Shower drains to EN 1253 usually have a 50 mm water seal, and it evaporates if nothing runs into the drain for weeks. Oatey advises pouring about 4 litres of water once a month into rarely used drains. If the smell returns sooner, the drain’s seal may be damaged or cracked.
  3. Only at the toilet, stronger after flushing Look at the connection at the back of the toilet, where it enters the pipe: damp, a dark ring on the floor or hardened mortar means the seal is not holding. Oatey names a connection flange fitted too low as one cause, because the gasket cannot press tight against it.
  4. After washing clothes Check how the drain hose enters the drain. The manufacturer (AEG) requires the end of the hose to be ventilated: the inner diameter of the drain pipe at least 38 mm and larger than the outer diameter of the hose, and the end not submerged in water. A hose wrapped tightly with tape, or a hose that goes straight into the pipe without a trap, lets air from the pipe come back.
  5. When several hoses enter one trap under the sink See whether the drain hose from the bathroom is also connected into the kitchen sink’s trap. LBN 221-15 forbids connecting appliances located in different rooms to one trap.
  6. All the time, in all rooms and also in the stairwell If all the traps are full and the smell is not tied to any appliance, the cause may be in the shared riser or the basement’s horizontal drain. That is the building’s common property, so report it to your building manager.

Why the water in a trap disappears

A few centimetres of water sit in the trap’s bend, and they separate the room from the sewer air. This water slowly evaporates, especially in a warm, dry room. While the appliance is in use, the water is renewed each time. When it is not used, for example a guest shower, a second sink or the drain by the washing machine, the trap is empty after a few weeks.

Before a long absence, Oatey advises pouring 1–2 tablespoons of mineral oil into each trap. The oil floats on the water and slows the evaporation. After you return, running the water as usual is enough.

A toilet in a Soviet series block: the toilet at a cast-iron riser

In Iļģuciems the first standard blocks of the 1950s were brick buildings of series 303, followed by series 316/318 and five-storey series 464 blocks. In their separate toilet, the toilet is connected to the riser that runs in the boxed-in riser duct in the toilet. If the toilet was replaced at some point and the connection was sealed with mortar or a temporary cuff, over the years a gap forms there and air comes through.

The toilet connection is usually the flat owner’s job, but a crack in the riser itself is the building’s matter. How the law draws this line is described on the page drain cleaning in Iļģuciems.

Rule (checked 16.11.2026). LBN 221-15: several appliances located in different rooms must not be connected to one trap.

When a bucket of water is not enough

If the smell comes from the toilet connection, the toilet is taken off, the riser socket is cleaned and the toilet is connected with a new cuff: replacing a toilet or connecting a new one is from €50. If there is no trap or the old plastic pipe to the riser has cracked, replacing the flat’s drain pipe is from €30 per connection point. Switching a washing machine to a drain connection with a trap is a machine connection, from €35.

If you cannot find the cause yourself, the plumber’s call-out and inspection is from €40. All prices are labour only; materials are extra.

Questions residents ask

We are going away for a month. How do we avoid coming back to a smelly flat?

A month is longer than the 2–4 weeks in which a trap can dry out. Before leaving, pour 1–2 tablespoons of mineral oil into each trap, as Oatey advises, and run water into the drains when you return.

The smell comes from the washing machine drum. Is the machine to blame?

Often the drain connection is to blame, not the machine. If the end of the drain hose is submerged in water or wrapped tightly into a pipe without a trap, the pipe’s air and water can get back into the drum. The manufacturer (AEG) requires a ventilated hose end.

The smell is also in the stairwell and at the neighbours'. Whom do we report it to?

Then the cause is most likely in the shared riser or the basement. That is the building’s common property, so it is usually reported to the building manager, and the building organises the inspection.
