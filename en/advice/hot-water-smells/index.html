Water heater smells - why the hot water stinks of rotten eggs

Water from a water heater smells of rotten eggs when hydrogen sulphide forms in the water. If the smell is only in the hot water, specialists at the University of Georgia (USA) name the water heater’s magnesium anode as a possible cause, because it reacts with the sulphates in the water. If the cold water smells too, the water heater is not to blame.

Five checks at the taps and the thermostat

  1. Cold tap Open only the cold water and smell it. If it smells too, the smell comes from the water supply, not from the water heater.
  2. Hot tap with the water heater Open the hot water from the water heater. If only that smells, the smell forms in the tank.
  3. The building’s hot water In a flat where the water heater is a backup, compare it with the building’s hot water. If the building’s hot water smells too, that is a question for your building manager.
  4. Thermostat setting Look at how many degrees the thermostat is set to and write it down. If the water heater has run for months on a lukewarm setting, compare it with the temperatures given below.
  5. How long the water has stood Remember whether the water heater and the taps have gone unused for weeks, for example after a holiday, or for a backup water heater between hot water outages.

Hydrogen sulphide forms at the magnesium anode

The magnesium anode in the tank wears away on purpose: it protects the steel from rust. The University of Georgia’s extension service explains that the anode can react with the sulphates in the water, and then hydrogen sulphide forms. People notice it as an unpleasant smell when the concentration in the water exceeds 0.5 ppm.

According to this source, the fix is to replace the anode with an aluminium or zinc anode. If the water is kept above about 65 °C, the service advises an aluminium anode only.

Temperature and standing water

The British Health and Safety Executive (HSE) advises storing hot water in tanks at 60 °C or hotter and distributing it at no less than 50 °C. This is a British recommendation, not a Latvian rule, but it shows that a water heater left lukewarm for long periods is not in a good state.

Because of legionella, AS “Rīgas siltums” (Riga’s district heating company) advises running rarely used taps and showers for a few minutes at least once a week, and in buildings with hot water tanks periodically heating the water above 55 degrees. Checked 16.11.2026.

In buildings with a modernised automated individual heat substation, the building’s hot water is 50–55 degrees according to Rīgas siltums. If the building’s hot water smells in such a building, the water heater’s anode does not explain it.

A backup water heater that stands for months

In a flat with the building’s hot water, the water heater is often switched on only on outage days. In Bolderāja, for example, hot water comes from the Rīgas siltums boiler house at Gobas iela 33a, and on the day its heating network is tested there is no hot water. For the rest of the year the water in a backup water heater stands still.

The manufacturer (Atlantic) advises draining the water heater if the power will be off for longer than two months or the room risks frost. A drained water heater stands without water and is refilled before the next use. If the water heater stays full, the Rīgas siltums hygiene advice against legionella applies: run the water for a few minutes once a week and periodically heat it above 55 degrees. This is advice against bacteria, not a proven remedy for the smell.

If the smell returns after every heating, the cause is probably in the tank, and the anode needs to be checked.

When the smell is a job for a plumber

If only the water heater’s hot water smells and changing the temperature does not help, the tank has to be opened: the sediment is removed and the anode inspected. This is done in water heater descaling, which costs from €75 per water heater. Replacing the anode with one of another material is a separate part. If a damaged heating element or thermostat turns up during descaling, that is already a water heater repair, and its price depends on the damaged part.

What helps in the request: whether only the water heater’s hot water smells, the temperature setting, how long the water heater has stood, the manufacturer from the label, and whether the anode has ever been changed. A photo of the label helps to see which anode the tank has from the factory and which one can be fitted instead.

Labour only; materials are extra. Whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work.

Questions residents ask

Can the anode simply be taken out to make the smell go away?

No. The anode protects the tank from rust, so a water heater is not left without one. The University of Georgia source advises replacing the magnesium anode with an aluminium or zinc anode.

Our building has a new heat substation, but the hot water still smells. Is the water heater to blame?

If the building’s hot water smells, not the water heater’s water, the water heater is not to blame. Ask your building manager about the quality of the building’s hot water.

Can the smell come back after descaling?

It can, if the tank has a magnesium anode again and the water contains sulphates. That is why, during descaling, it is worth talking about the anode material, not only about limescale.
