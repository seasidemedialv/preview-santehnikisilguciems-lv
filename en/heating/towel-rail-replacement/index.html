Heated towel rail replacement - water, mixed or electric

Replacing a heated towel rail in Iļģuciems and the surrounding area starts with a choice of what heats the new rail: the building’s pipe, the pipe and an electric element together, or electricity alone.

In a Soviet series block bathroom the old rail is usually part of the building’s hot water circulation, so work on it is agreed with the building manager. Replacement on the existing connection costs from €80, labour only; materials are extra.

The rail is a visible part of the circulation loop

In a consultation on the LV portāls (Latvia’s official legal information portal), the heated towel rail is described as a small, visible section of the building’s hot water circulation. Hot water circulates through this pipe past all the floors, so removing the rail may mean briefly stopping the hot water flow in the riser. That is why the LV portāls writes that any work on such a rail must be agreed with the building manager.

Iļģuciems has mostly series 316/318 silicate-brick blocks and series 464 five-storey panel blocks. If the rails in a block are connected to the heating, that does not yet mean there is no hot water circulation there: the LV portāls states this separately too. Which pipe your own rail is connected to can be seen from photos of the supply pipes, or ask your building manager. If you are replacing radiators at the same time, the procedure is described on the page radiator replacement.

In blocks run by Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks (Riga’s municipal building manager), a similar rail in the same place is replaced on application, but moving it needs a design. Other building managers may have a different procedure. Checked 16.11.2026.

From €80: the work at the wall and what stays outside

  • Included: disconnecting and removing the old rail.
  • Included: fixing the new rail and connecting it to the existing hot water or heating pipe, with a bypass and valves where they are needed.
  • Included: checking the joints once the pipe has water in it again.
  • Not included: the rail itself, the electric element and the fittings.
  • Not included: the power supply, the residual current device and the connection box, which an electrician installs.
  • Not included: the building manager’s work on the riser and a design for moving the rail.

Three ways a towel rail can heat

Type What heats it What the manufacturer or building manager requires
Water-fed rail The building’s hot water or heating pipe Work is agreed with the building manager (LV portāls)
Mixed mode Heating pipe and an electric element at the bottom Zehnder: the element only in a rail connected to district heating; the return valve always open
Electric Electricity only Stelrad: outside zone 1 of the bathroom, no plug

Mixed mode lets the rail heat with the element even when there is no hot water in the pipe. Zehnder intends its element only for water-fed rails on district heating, so it is not meant for a rail on a hot water pipe.

Connecting a heated towel rail in mixed mode

  1. Element at the bottom, vertical Zehnder requires the element to be fitted vertically in the bottom of the rail, with the control unit at the lower end.
  2. Height against element length The rail must be at least 1.5 times taller than the length of the element.
  3. Element output The electrical output must not exceed 110% of the rail’s water output at dT=50; the manufacturer recommends at least 60%.
  4. Return valve open The element heats the water, and water expands. That is why Zehnder requires the radiator’s return valve to be always open.
  5. System limits Zehnder states: system pressure up to 4 bar, temperature up to 95 °C, glycol concentration up to 20%.

The plumber hangs the rail and connects it to the water. An electrician makes the element’s electrical connection.

Installing a heated towel rail in a children’s bathroom

  • Height: for an electrically heated rail, Zehnder recommends the lowest heating tube at least 600 mm above the floor if there are small children in the house.
  • Temperature: for a chrome rail with an electric element, Zehnder recommends a surface temperature of 65 °C or lower.
  • Electricity: the supply needs a residual current device of no more than 30 mA and a connection box at least 25 cm above the floor (Zehnder).
  • Location: Stelrad places an electric rail outside zone 1, so that a person in the bath or shower cannot reach the controls.

If the old water-fed rail hung right at the edge of the bath, the electric one may need another location. Moving it is then a separate job.

Photos and measurements from the bathroom wall

  • Photos: the whole rail and both supply pipes close up.
  • The distance between the supply pipes in millimetres.
  • Which type you choose: water, mixed or electric.
  • Whether there is an electrical connection nearby and whether there are small children in the house.
  • The building’s address or series, and the building manager.

Two rails in a private house: the total

Example: in a private house with two bathrooms, two water-fed rails on the heating circuit are replaced. 2 × from €80 = at least €160 for labour. The rails, elements and the electrician’s work are not in this total.

Whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work.

Questions residents ask

Can an electric element be fitted in a water-fed rail so that it also heats without the heating?

Zehnder allows this only in rails connected to district heating, and requires the return valve to stay always open. This kind of element is not intended for a rail on a hot water riser; there you can consider a separate electric rail.

How high must the rail hang if there is a small child in the house?

For an electrically heated rail, Zehnder recommends the lowest heating tube at least 600 mm above the floor, and for a chrome model a surface temperature of 65 °C or lower.

Can an electric rail be placed next to the bath?

Stelrad requires it to be installed outside zone 1 of the bathroom, so that the controls cannot be reached from the bath or shower, and does not allow it to be connected with a plug. An electrician makes the connection.
