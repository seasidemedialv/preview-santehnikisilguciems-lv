Two valves in the boxed-in duct, and in a private house the main one is in the chamber

In a Soviet series block flat, the cold and hot water branches start at the riser in the boxed-in riser duct in the toilet. Each branch has the flat’s valve by the meter: usually before the meter, sometimes another one after it. Closing both stops the water only in your flat. The Iļģuciems residential area was planned in 1968 from five-storey large-panel sections, and in a five-storey block one riser is usually shared by five flats one above the other.

Under Article 3 of the Apartment Ownership Law, the flat’s separate property includes the utility lines as far as the common-property risers. So a leak after your valve is usually repaired by the flat owner, and the riser by the building, unless your building’s decisions and contracts provide otherwise.

A private house usually has one main valve: in the meter chamber or where the pipe enters the house. According to Rīgas ūdens, your responsibility usually starts at the outside of the meter chamber wall; for a house without a chamber the boundary runs along the plot boundary or the red line. If the leak is in the section between the chamber and the house, the valve in the house does not stop it, and the one in the chamber has to be closed.

How to turn off the water in a flat if the valve is old

The US water utility Brunswick & Topsham Water District writes that homes from the 1970s and earlier usually have gate valves: a metal plate is raised or lowered with a handwheel, turned several times. In newer homes the standard is a ball valve, closed by turning the lever through 90 degrees. In a home where the toilet duct has not been opened for decades, the first kind may still be there.

A gate valve that nobody has moved for years tends to seize in an emergency. WaterSafe, the plumbers' register in Great Britain, advises closing and opening the main valve a couple of times a year so that it does not seize. If the valve is closed but water still comes, do not force it: report the riser to the building manager and write in the form that the valve does not hold.

Water on the floor: four actions before the form

  1. Valves at the meters Close the cold and hot water valves in the toilet duct; in a private house, the incoming valve in the chamber or in the house.
  2. Electricity Do not touch sockets and appliances standing in water. Switch off the fuses at the board only if you can reach the board over a dry floor.
  3. Water heater If the water heater leaks or there is water near its socket, first switch off its electricity, then close the cold water supply to the water heater.
  4. Neighbour and belongings Warn the neighbour on the floor below, lift documents and electronics off the floor and photograph the place where it leaks.

The building manager shuts the riser, the building decides on replacement

An explanation on the LV portāls (Latvia’s official legal information portal) states that the building manager can deal with an emergency at once, without authorisation from the flat owners, for example by putting a clamp on a burst pipe. Replacing the riser, by contrast, is decided by all the owners together. So a clamp is a temporary solution, and riser replacement in a flat is the next step after the building’s decision.

If the building manager has carried out an extraordinary repair, for example on a riser branch, under item 23 of Cabinet Regulation No. 408 it must report the costs in writing to the building’s authorised person within two weeks. For damage from a burst riser, the LV portāls names the flat owners as the first to be responsible, and the building manager if it took on inspection and repair in the contract and did not do it; it advises contacting the insurer first. In your particular building, ask your building manager.

When the water is turned back on

After the repair, open the valve slowly, not in one pull. WaterSafe advises restoring the water gradually, inspecting the joints at once and again after 24 hours, because a small leak often appears only after a while. If after an emergency brownish water comes from the taps on your street, let it run until it is clear; that is no longer an emergency job.

What not to do yourself in an emergency

  • Do not turn the riser valves in the cellar: the whole vertical is left without water, and that is the building manager’s decision.
  • Do not force a stuck gate valve with a long spanner: the stem can snap, and then it leaks even more.
  • Do not switch the water heater on until the tank is full again after the repair: a heating element in a dry tank fails.
  • Do not wrap tape round a burst pipe or open the valve at full pressure: tape does not hold such pressure for long.

Questions residents ask

The building manager put a clamp on our riser. How long can it stay?

A clamp stops the emergency but is not a replacement. Replacing the riser is decided by the flat owners together, so ask your building manager whether the building plans to replace the whole vertical or only the section on your floor.

An extraordinary repair on the riser appeared on the bill. Why does the whole building pay for it?

The LV portāls explains that the systems serving the building are common property, and the building manager can ask all the flat owners to cover the cost of an extraordinary repair of an unforeseeable fault. The costs must be reported in writing to the authorised person within two weeks.

A ball valve has a lever. How do I know it is closed?

A ball valve closes with a quarter turn. When the lever sits across the pipe, the valve is shut; when it runs along the pipe, it is open. If the lever turns but water still comes, the valve is faulty.

There is no water from the taps, but nothing leaks. Is it an emergency?

If the neighbours have no water either, it is usually the street network or the building manager’s work. Rīgas ūdens maintains the city system up to the ownership boundary, so ask your building manager first. A plumber is needed if only your flat has no water.
