Sand on the dune, wet ground on the flat

The underside of an outdoor water pipe must lie at least 0.5 m deeper than the point where the ground can cool to 0 °C (LBN 222-15, clause 164). In Riga the standard freezing depth once in 100 years is 120 cm for clay ground, and for sandy ground it is multiplied by 1.2, so about 144 cm (LBN 003-19). For a house on a dune, sand is exactly that case.

For a yard sewer line the rule is different. A gravity line outdoors may be laid 0.3 m higher than the standard frost limit, but at least 0.7 m of earth must remain above the top of the pipe. If the line lies at 0.7 m depth or shallower, it is protected both from frost and from the weight of a vehicle (LBN 223-15).

If the depth of an old pipe is not known, you can see it only where the pipe comes out of the ground: at the incoming supply pipe and at the house foundation. In the form write whether these places are accessible and how many metres lie between them.

A leak under the lawn is not always visible

A pipe that leaks in the ground often shows itself above it: the soil stays wet, the lawn or paving sinks, in a dry summer one strip of grass is greener than the rest, or the pressure at the taps changes. The British water company Yorkshire Water reminds us that not every outdoor leak is visible.

  1. Close all the taps in the house Also the supplies to the water heater and washing machine, if they have their own valves.
  2. Look at the water meter If its reading keeps changing, water is going somewhere. This does not tell you the place, but it tells you that there is a leak.
  3. Close the valve in the house and look again If the meter is in a chamber at the incoming supply pipe and still turns, the section between the chamber meter and the house is leaking, that is, in the yard.

It is worth knowing the position of the main valve in advance. In a Riga private house it is usually in the meter chamber or where the pipe enters the house. According to Rīgas ūdens, your responsibility usually starts at the outside of the meter chamber wall, so a leak in the yard section is stopped only by the valve in the chamber, not the one in the house (see emergency plumber).

Questions residents ask

My address is in Spilve, but in Babīte parish. Is this page about me?

No. This page is about the Riga neighbourhood of Spilve, which borders Iļģuciems and Imanta. In Babīte parish of Mārupe municipality there is another Spilve. In the form enter the full address, and on the call the plumber confirms whether we travel there.

The pipe runs under the driveway. Does the ground freeze deeper there?

Yes, it can. The freezing depth observed over the last 20 years has usually been 40–60 cm, but snow cover reduces it by 20–40%, so under a cleared driveway or path the ground freezes deeper (LSM, 2025). If the line lies there at 0.7 m depth or shallower, it must also be protected from the weight of a vehicle.

The house stood empty for a long time. How do I turn the water on again?

The British plumbers' institute CIPHE advises first unscrewing the aerators from the taps and taking off the shower hoses. Then turn the water on: at first cold only, then hot, tap by tap from the one nearest the incoming supply pipe to the furthest, until the pipes no longer carry the old water. If a joint then leaks, write about it in the form.
