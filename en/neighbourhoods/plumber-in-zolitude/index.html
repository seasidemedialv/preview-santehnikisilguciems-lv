In the north, between Zolitūdes iela and Endzelīna iela, there is a high-rise estate of series 119 blocks, designed in Latvia, and a few series 104 panel blocks of 12–16 floors with a single stairwell. The centre and south of the neighbourhood are dominated by detached houses. In flats the work often concerns risers and days without hot water; in detached houses, the house’s own incoming supply pipe, yard sewer line and water heater.

A day without hot water and a backup water heater

The Zolitūde residential area is in a zone where there is no hot water during the hydraulic test of the Imanta and Zasulauks heat substation networks. How this day goes is described in the advice article no hot water in the flat. Anyone who needs hot water on that day too hangs a small water heater in the flat, connected to the cold water pipe.

A backup water heater sits switched off for most of the year, and that is its weak point. AS “Rīgas siltums” advises that in buildings with hot water tanks the water be heated periodically above 55 degrees. The manufacturer Ariston uses an anti-legionella cycle in its electronic water heaters: the water is heated to 60 °C and held for one hour, and if the water heater constantly runs below 55 °C, the cycle is repeated every 30 days.

Hot water carries another risk too. Ariston warns that water hotter than 50 °C scalds instantly, and it endangers children and elderly people most. So the manufacturer recommends a thermostatic mixing valve on the hot water outlet of the water heater.

One riser, sixteen floors

In a series 104 tower the riser connects flats that lie directly one above another. If a section is replaced on one floor, the water is shut off for the whole riser, that is, for all the flats on that vertical from bottom to top. So the work is first agreed with the building manager, who also shuts off the riser.

The Residential Property Management Law states that the building’s owner is responsible for managing the building, and in an apartment block the owner is made up of the flat owners. A riser is common property: unless the building’s documents say otherwise, the decision about it is taken jointly, and for details ask your building manager.

For a soil stack, the price of a section includes the transitions to the neighbouring sections above and below. If the neighbour’s section is of a different material, the transition is the most important point of the job. In the form write which floor you live on, how many floors the building has and what material the sections above and below you are made of, if you can see it in the boxed-in riser duct in the toilet.

Questions residents ask

On the test day the hot water did not come back in the evening. What does that mean?

AS “Rīgas siltums” restores hot water that evening if no faults were found in the test. If the water does not come back, most likely something in the network has to be repaired. Rīgas siltums informs building managers about it, so ask your building manager.

A backup water heater stands switched off from one test to the next. What should I do before I switch it on?

AS “Rīgas siltums” advises running rarely used taps for a few minutes at least once a week. You can do the same with the hot tap on the water heater. The British HSE advises keeping hot water in the tank at a minimum of 60 °C, and at the tap it should reach at least 50 °C. If the water heater has stood for a long time and the water smells, see water heater descaling.

In a two-storey house there is one water supply for several flats. Who replaces the pipe to the house?

Under the Law on Water Management Services, the supplier’s network and the user’s system meet at the ownership boundary, which in an apartment block is the flat owners' common property. So usually all the owners replace the pipe from the boundary to the house together. How this is divided in your building, ask your building manager.
