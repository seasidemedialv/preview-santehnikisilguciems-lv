Who owns the pipe: a series-block flat and a private house

In a flat, the boundary is set by the Apartment Ownership Law. Under Article 3, the utility lines up to the common-property risers belong to the separate property, as does the toilet, shower and bath equipment. Under Article 4, the riser and the basement horizontal pipes are the building’s common property. So the trap and the branch up to the boxed-in riser duct in the toilet are usually cleaned and paid for by the flat owner, while the riser is organised by the building manager, unless the building’s documents say otherwise.

Iļģuciems also has nine-storey series 602 blocks and 12-storey Czech-design buildings. There, one riser serves more flats than in a five-storey block, and a riser blockage affects more neighbours. If the riser cleanout is in your toilet’s boxed-in duct, Article 10 of the law obliges you to let in a specialist authorised by the building manager.

In a private house the boundary is at the street. “Rīgas ūdens” maintains the city network up to the ownership boundary, and most often that is the outer edge of the meter chamber wall. If there is no chamber, the boundary is usually the street’s red line, that is, the plot boundary. Everything from there to the house and inside it is the owner’s responsibility. “Rīgas ūdens” prepares an exact boundary diagram on request.

Cable thickness depends on the pipe

Under the Latvian Building Code LBN 221-15, the drain connection for a kitchen sink, bath and shower is at least 40 mm, and for a toilet 100 mm. One cable does not suit all pipes. The manufacturer (REMS) gives this choice for its machines:

  • 8 mm cable: for pipes of 20–50 mm (up to 75 mm), up to 10 m long;
  • 16 mm cable: for pipes of 25–125 mm, up to 40 m;
  • 22 mm cable: for pipes of 50–150 mm, up to 70 m;
  • 32 mm cable: for pipes of 50–250 mm, up to 70 m.

For a flat’s sink branch a thin cable is enough, but a private house’s yard line needs a thicker and longer one. REMS recommends first sending in a straight drill and judging from the debris pulled out what is in the pipe: grease, hair, sand or wipes. At a blockage the cable is advanced a centimetre at a time, because a jammed cable can snap.

Drain jetting, when grease stays on the walls

The cable pokes through a channel, but grease and limescale stay on the pipe walls. For removing grease and limescale REMS names the chain cleaner as the key tool: for plastic pipes, chains with smooth rings are used, for cast-iron pipes, ones with pointed links. The other option is high-pressure water jetting, which costs from €60 an hour, at least 1 h.

If a kitchen branch keeps clogging again and again and the pipe is old, it may work out cheaper to replace the branch up to the riser: from €30 per connection point.

What the drains accept and what they do not

The Latvian Water and Sewerage Companies' Association recalls the “ČKP law”: only urine, faeces and toilet paper may enter the drains. Wet wipes do not break down in water like paper. They catch on pipe bends and joints, and in blocks of flats and private houses they cause blocked pipes, smells and waste water in the rooms. A smell without a blockage has its own causes, which we describe in the article sewer smell in a flat.

A private house’s basement below street level: the backwater valve

If a private house’s basement has a shower, a washing machine or a floor drain, waste water from a full street or yard line can come back right there. A sewer backwater valve is fitted against this. The manufacturer (HL Hutterer & Lechner) states for its electronic valve a sensor that reacts to water and an electric motor that closes the valve; a battery runs it for up to 4 days if the power fails.

  • The valve is fitted horizontally, with a fall of at least 1 % towards the drains.
  • At least a 1 m straight section is needed before and after the valve.
  • Rainwater must not be routed through this valve.
  • The manufacturer recommends having few fixtures below the backwater level and one more toilet above it.

In Šampēteris, “Rīgas ūdens” built new sewer networks within the last 15 years, and on Dzirciema iela the networks were renewed by the trenchless method. A new street pipe does not mean a new yard line: it stays as it was.

What not to do yourself

  • Do not pour a second dose of caustic cleaner on top of the first. The Swedish Chemicals Agency (KemI) recommends clearing a blockage mechanically first, and if the product is left in the pipe, you must tell the plumber: it contains sodium or potassium hydroxide.
  • Do not clear a blocked pump toilet (macerator) yourself. The UK Chartered Institute of Plumbing and Heating Engineering (CIPHE) recommends calling a plumber in that case.
  • Do not force a hired cable or hold it with bare hands: a jammed cable can snap, and a dirty cable is a health risk, which is why REMS requires protective gloves.

What the drain cleaning price depends on

Clearing a blockage in a flat or house costs from €50 per call-out. Cleaning a yard line costs from €50 and includes the first hour; longer work is charged by the hour. High-pressure jetting: from €60 for each hour. The total is changed by the pipe’s diameter and length, where the line can be opened (a cleanout behind tiles or a chamber in the yard) and what is in the pipe, including any chemicals poured in. Labour only; materials are extra.

Whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work.

Questions residents ask

I poured in a drain cleaner, but the water still stands. What now?

Do not pour any more. Open a window, because CIPHE warns that these products give off fumes. In the form, write which product and how much was poured in, because the plumber needs to know before working with a cable.

The street sewer was renewed recently. Is my yard line new too?

No. “Rīgas ūdens” renews the network up to the ownership boundary, usually the meter chamber or the plot boundary. The line from there to the house stays the owner’s responsibility.

Can I flush wipes labelled “flushable”?

CIPHE states that any wipes block a toilet, including those called flushable. Put them in the bin.

The building manager wants to clean the riser through my toilet duct. Do I have to let them in?

Article 10 of the Apartment Ownership Law obliges a flat owner to let specialists authorised by the building manager into the flat to do whatever work the building’s utilities need in order to keep working. Agree the time with the building manager.
