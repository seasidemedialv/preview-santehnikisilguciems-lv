Who owns the supply: a series-block flat against a private house

In a series-block flat your part usually starts at the riser. The flat’s valve is at the water meter, usually before the meter, sometimes after it. The toilet, sink, taps and pipes up to the riser are the flat’s equipment, unless the building’s documents say otherwise. The riser in the toilet box is common property of the building, and the building manager shuts it off. If the flat’s valve no longer holds the water, a fixture cannot be replaced until the whole riser is shut off, and that affects the neighbours above and below you.

In a private house everything after the incoming supply pipe belongs to the owner. The water is shut off at the main valve by the incoming pipe, and nobody else is left without water. The drains go through the yard sewer line to the city chamber or a holding tank, so a blockage in a ground-floor toilet is usually to be looked for in the house or the yard, not at a neighbour’s.

Bathroom plumbing in a small room

In a series-block bathroom every centimetre is taken: the bath along one wall, the sink by the door, the washing machine under the sink or next to the bath. So before buying a new fixture, measure not only the space but also where the water supply pipes and the drain come out of the wall. That decides whether the fixture can be connected in the old place or the supply has to be moved.

There are guide heights. If the old sink hung too low or too high, the right height can be chosen for the new one, if the supply pipes allow it.

Guide heights for a flat under the Latvian Building Code LBN 221-15: hand basin edge 800 mm above the floor, a sink next to the bath 850 mm, wall tap 200 mm higher than the basin, bath tap at 800 mm height. Tolerance is up to ±20 mm. Checked 16.11.2026.

A separate toilet with the riser in a box

In a narrow toilet room there is usually a box or a panel behind the toilet through which the water and drain risers run. Both you and the building manager must be able to reach them, so the box must stay openable even after a new toilet or a concealed frame. The toilet’s connection to the drains must not be narrower than 100 mm.

If you are replacing the toilet, see toilet installation. If the toilet is the old one but the cistern or a connection leaks, first read toilet repair: often one part is enough.

Plumbing installation for several fixtures at once

If you are renovating the whole bathroom, the fixtures go in a set order. First the pipes and the drain, because they stay in the wall or the floor, then the tiles. The manufacturers Ravak and Kaldewei put the bath or shower tray in at the finishing stage, when the tiles are done. The sink, the toilet and the mixer taps are connected last.

  • Bath replacement: an acrylic or steel bath in place of the old one and how each is fitted.
  • Shower cabin installation: the tray material, the joint and the thermostat requirements.
  • Sink installation: on the wall in a bathroom or in a worktop in a kitchen.
  • Mixer tap replacement: a cartridge or the whole tap, eccentric fittings and hoses.
  • Washing machine connection: the tap, the drain, the feet and a dishwasher in a kitchen.

Each room gets its own trap: LBN 221-15 does not allow a kitchen and a bathroom sink to be joined into one trap.

Jobs better not started yourself

  • Removing a toilet without checking that the flat’s valve holds. If it leaks through, water will come through the open supply, and only the building manager can shut off the riser.
  • Chiselling out an old cast-iron socket. The socket is part of the riser, and a crack in it becomes a problem for the whole building.
  • Tiling over the riser box without an opening. In an emergency the box will have to be broken out.
  • Tightening a tap by force on an old steel pipe. A rusted thread can break off inside the wall.

What the price depends on

The labour price changes with whether the fixture goes in the old place directly on the existing supply pipes, or whether a pipe has to be extended, the drain pipe to the riser replaced or a supply moved. A concealed toilet with a frame is bigger work than a toilet on the floor. If the old flat valve leaks through, replacing it is a separate job before the fixture is replaced, and it needs the riser to be shut off. Prices are labour only; materials are extra. Whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work. All prices are on the page plumbing prices.

Questions residents ask

Can a new toilet be connected to the old cast-iron socket?

Usually it can, if the socket is not cracked and its inside can be cleaned. A rubber collar or an adapter goes between the toilet outlet and the socket. If the socket is damaged, a section of the riser has to be replaced, and that is agreed with the building manager.

I bought the fixture myself. Can it be installed?

Yes, the price list has labour prices without the fixture. In the form write the make and model, so the plumber knows what fixings and supply connections come in the set.

Can a toilet, a sink and a tap be replaced at one visit?

They can. Write all the fixtures in the form. On the call the plumber talks through the job and the price with you: per fixture or by the hour.

What should I prepare in the flat before the work?

Clear the space around the fixture and the access to the flat’s valves. If the riser box is screwed shut, say so in the form.
