The manufacturer decides when to do it for the first time, and manufacturers' intervals differ by as much as three times. A water heater that takes longer and longer to heat up is also a sign that the time has come.

What the descaling price includes and what it does not

The price from €75 includes:

  • draining the water from the tank;
  • removing the limescale;
  • inspecting the heating element and the anode.

Charged separately:

  • a new anode and a new flange gasket, if the old one is no longer usable;
  • replacing the heating element or the thermostat, which is already water heater repair;
  • taking the water heater off the wall, if there is no room above or in front of it for servicing.

Tank open: step by step

  1. Power and water The water heater is disconnected from the power, the cold water supply is closed and the tank is emptied.
  2. Flange The service cover and the flange with the heating element are removed. That is exactly why free space is needed in front of the cover.
  3. Limescale The loose limescale is removed from the bottom of the tank. The manufacturer (Atlantic) warns against scraping or knocking limescale stuck to the tank walls, as this can damage the coating.
  4. Anode The magnesium anode is inspected and measured. Atlantic says to replace it if its diameter is below 10 mm.
  5. Back together The flange goes back with a sound gasket, the tank is refilled, the joints are checked and only then is the power switched on.

What to write about the water heater in the request

  • The manufacturer and volume from the label on the casing.
  • How many years the water heater has been on the wall and whether it has ever been opened.
  • Whether it has an anode indicator light and whether the light is on.
  • How long you now wait for hot water compared with before.
  • Flat or private house, the floor, and whether there is free space in front of the cover.
  • A photo of the water heater and its underside.

Descaling a water heater starts with the temperature

The manufacturer (Thermex) states that a longer heating time points to deposits on the heating element. Limescale on the thermostat tube can also cause the thermal cut-out to trip often. Gorenje advises keeping the thermostat at the “eco” position, where the water is at 55 °C and limescale deposits and heat losses are smaller.

If the hot water smells of hydrogen sulphide

If only the hot water smells, the University of Georgia Extension says the cause can be a magnesium anode reacting with the sulphates in the water. That is why the anode is inspected during descaling. What to check beforehand is described in the article hot water smells.

Questions residents ask

Can limescale be scraped off the tank walls?

No. The manufacturer (Atlantic) warns against scraping or knocking limescale stuck to the walls, as this can damage the tank coating. Only the loose limescale is removed from the bottom.

Does the water heater have to come off the wall for descaling?

Not always. The flange with the heating element can be removed in place if there is free space in front of the cover. The manufacturer (Thermex) requires at least 30 cm from the protective cover to the nearest surface for this. If the cover butts against a wall or a washing machine, the water heater is taken down, and that is charged separately.

Do water heaters that hang only as a back-up need descaling too?

The manufacturer sets the interval by time since installation, not by days of use: Thermex after one year, Gorenje after about three. There is usually less limescale in a back-up water heater, though, because it heats less often.
