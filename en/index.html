Which buildings stand in Iļģuciems and what breaks in them

In 2025, 20,118 people lived in Iļģuciems, and multi-storey blocks are spread across the whole neighbourhood, except the northern part between the railway and Spilves iela. The buildings date from different decades, so the plumbing differs from one block to the next.

The street network is a separate matter. In the long-term plan of Rīgas ūdens, Iļģuciems is named among the neighbourhoods with the most water main failures, and across the networks of these neighbourhoods taken together as much as 40% of the pipe length is on the critical list. This concerns the street pipes up to the ownership boundary: the water utility maintains them, not the flat owner.

Hot water in Iļģuciems and the Grīva area comes from the Imanta and Zasulauks heating plant networks. When Rīgas siltums (Riga’s district heating company) runs a hydraulic test of these networks in summer, there is no hot water across the district, and a water heater in the flat then works as a back-up.

The water route from the meter to the water heater

In a series-block flat, water comes from the riser in the boxed-in duct in the toilet, passes through the shut-off valves and meters, and then runs through steel or already replaced plastic pipes to the taps. If a joint, a valve or a short piece of pipe leaks, stopping the leak costs from €40. If water is coming from the ceiling or you cannot find the valve, start with the emergency plumber page.

The water riser is common property of the building. Replacing its section on one floor costs from €110 per section, and it needs the building manager’s permission and the whole riser shut off: more on the riser replacement page. Old pipes inside a flat are described on the pipe repair in Iļģuciems page, and laying new pipe costs from €10/m.

Water heater installation costs from €70, water heater descaling from €75, and the price of water heater repair depends on the faulty part and on whether the water heater has to come off the wall. An overview of all water heaters is on the water heaters page.

Radiators in a building with Rīgas siltums and in a house with its own boiler

In a multi-flat building, a radiator is part of the whole riser, so the building manager sets the dates and the permission for replacing it. For example, Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks (Riga’s municipal building manager) lets residents of its buildings replace radiators after the heating season, up to 31 August. Rīgas siltums states in general that before a replacement the heating designer prepares documentation and agrees it with the manager, and the work is done when the riser can be isolated and emptied, roughly from 1 May to 1 October.

Radiator replacement procedure: AS “Rīgas siltums” (rs.lv) and Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks (rnparvaldnieks.lv), checked on 17.11.2026.

Radiator replacement costs from €75 per unit, heated towel rail replacement from €80. A leaking valve or a stuck thermostatic head is described on the radiator repair page. In a private house that has its own boiler the work can be planned at any time, and a sludgy system calls for heating system flushing: its price depends on the number of radiators and circuits. All heating topics together: heating and radiators.

Drains: from the kitchen trap to the cast-iron soil stack

A flat’s drain pipe runs from the sink, bath or toilet to the soil stack. If only one fixture does not drain, the fault is usually in the trap or in your own branch pipe: clearing a blockage in a flat costs from €50. If water rises in the flat below, the blockage is in the soil stack or in the horizontal drain in the basement, and the building manager must be told.

Grease in a kitchen drain sometimes does not clear with a cable, and then high-pressure jetting helps (from €60 per hour, at least 1 h). Cleaning a private house’s yard sewer line up to the city inspection chamber costs from €50. Everything about drains is on the drain cleaning page, and replacing a cast-iron soil stack section costs from €100 per section on one floor.

The separate toilet and the narrow bathroom

In five-storey blocks the toilet is usually separate and so narrow that the model has to be chosen by the direction of its outlet and the depth of the room. Toilet installation costs from €50, and toilet repair, when the flush mechanism leaks or does not fill, from €35.

In the bathroom and kitchen: mixer tap replacement from €25, sink installation from €40, bath replacement from €60, shower cabin installation from €125 and washing machine connection from €35. If there are several small jobs, a plumber for small jobs by the hour is cheaper, from €25/h. All fixtures in one place: plumbing fixtures.

Questions residents ask

How soon will someone answer after I send the form?

After you submit the form, we contact you within 5 minutes. The call comes to the phone number you entered in the form.

Can I book a job at night or on a Sunday?

Yes, we work also at night and at weekends. You can send the form at any time.

Can the riser in the toilet duct be replaced without the building manager?

No. A riser is shared by the whole building, so the building manager’s permission is needed, and the water must be shut off for the whole riser. Who pays is usually decided by the owners' association: Article 13 of the Apartment Ownership Law provides that each owner pays for replacing common property utilities according to their share.

Why is there no hot water in Iļģuciems for a day in summer?

In summer Rīgas siltums tests the heating networks zone by zone, and Iļģuciems is connected to the Imanta and Zasulauks heating plant networks. The test lasts one day, and building managers are informed about the interruption. More in the article no hot water in the flat.

Do the prices include materials?

No. All prices are labour only; materials are extra. The full list is on the plumbing prices page.

Do you also go to a private house with a meter pit and its own boiler?

Yes. In a private house, responsibility for the pipe usually starts at the outer edge of the meter pit wall. Everything beyond it is yours to look after: the circuit of the heating boiler, the water heater, the yard sewer line and the incoming supply pipe.
