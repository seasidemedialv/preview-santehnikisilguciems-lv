Old yards, restored houses, a 1970s student campus and the business-centre development in the north of the island stand side by side. In an old house or yard you first have to find out where the pipes run, so many jobs start with an inspection and continue as hourly work. Outside the house you need to know where your responsibility ends and the city network begins.

Ķīpsala’s buildings: from fishermen’s yards to halls of residence

  • Old yards. The island of fishermen, seamen and pilots gave the streets their names: Matrožu, Tīklu, Enkuru, Kaiju, Zvejnieku, Loču and Ogļu. Almost every yard had ice cellars for storing fish and outbuildings made of planks from old sailing ships, and at least three such outbuildings have survived. For plumbing: if the water or drain line runs through a yard, its position must be known before digging.
  • Student campus. In the 1970s, in the formerly uninhabited part of the island, teaching buildings and halls of residence of the Riga Polytechnic Institute (now RTU) were built. For plumbing: a fault in a room or a suite is first reported to the building’s administration.
  • Business-centre development. It covers 20.5 ha in the north of the island and around Valdemāra iela, while new residential development is planned in the west of the island north of the exhibition complex. For plumbing: office kitchenettes, toilets and water heaters, whose connection is decided by the owner of the premises.
  • Restored houses. In the “Ķīpsalas kolekcija”, on the initiative of the architect Zaiga Gaile, buildings at Balasta dambis 60 and 60a, Ogļu iela 6 and the rental house of Eižens Laube built in 1909 at Zvejnieku iela 5a have been renewed. For plumbing: new pipes may be laid in boxes or in the floor, so before drilling you must find out where they run.

It is about 1.9 km from the centre of Iļģuciems (our map reading), but the island has no land border with Iļģuciems: bridges lead to Ķīpsala from Dzirciems, Āgenskalns and the Centre.

Water around the island and sand ground under it

In the west of the island is the Zunds canal, 50–200 m wide and 3–7 m deep, and in the south-west the 40–50 m wide Āzene. The bank along Balasta dambis was reinforced with boulders in 1885, and in 1891 the FG dam for the steamer pier was built at the end of Enkuru iela. The island itself formed as a sandbank of river-deposited sand with silty interlayers and became fully stable in the early 20th century, when the remains of the side channels were filled in.

Inside a flat this changes nothing. It matters when the fault is by the supply pipe or in the yard sewer line: in sandy ground the trench can collapse, so such work can be larger than the same work in firm ground. The plumber can state its scope only on site.

Rīgas ūdens: the customer is responsible for their water supply and sewer system from the ownership boundary to the building and inside it. If there is a meter chamber by the building, the boundary usually runs along the outer side of its wall. Where there is no chamber, the boundary is drawn along the plot boundary or the street’s red line. Checked 16.11.2026.

One street through the whole island

Ķīpsalas iela, Zvejnieku iela and Matrožu iela are in effect one street running through the island, and the terminus of bus 57 is on Matrožu iela in the north of the island. Street names change along the way, so in the form write the street and number exactly as they appear on the building and, if the entrance is from the yard, write that too.

If something large has to be brought, for example a bath or a large water heater, note the floor and whether the stairs are narrow or there is a lift. It affects how many people are needed and how long the work takes.

A restored house: what to find out before the work

Work inside a flat (mixer tap, toilet, sink, water heater) does not touch the facade or the stairwell. If a pipe has to be taken through a shared wall, the stairwell or the cellar, it is part of the building’s common property. The Apartment Ownership Law obliges the owner to treat common property with care, so first discuss such work with the building manager or the building’s owner.

The building’s common parts are usually looked after by the building manager: for example, in 2022 Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks (Riga’s municipal building manager) renovated and restored the entrance portal at Zvejnieku iela 9. If your building has special conditions for restored parts, the building manager knows them, not the plumber.

Inspection and hourly work in Ķīpsala

  • Call-out and inspection: from €40 per call-out. Whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work.
  • Hourly rate: from €25/h, the smallest paid time is usually 1 hour.
  • Stopping and repairing a leak: from €40 per job.
  • Laying a water pipe: from €10/m along a wall or floor; concealed laying in a wall costs more.

Example for an old building: 4 m of new pipe along a wall to the kitchen: 4 × from €10/m = at least €40 labour, materials are extra.

Questions residents ask

I live in a hall of residence on the island. Can I request a plumber myself?

A hall of residence’s plumbing is usually maintained by the building’s owner, so first report the fault to the administration. If it allows you to order the work yourself, fill in the form and write the building and the room number.

In an old building, can only one pipe be replaced rather than the whole water supply of the flat?

Yes. The section that leaks or has become clogged is replaced and joined to the existing pipes with transition fittings. If the inspection shows that the neighbouring sections are weak too, the plumber says so, but you decide.

Who is responsible for the water line between the building and the street?

According to Rīgas ūdens, the customer is responsible from the ownership boundary to the building. Usually that is the outer side of the meter chamber’s wall, and without a chamber the plot boundary or the street’s red line. In a multi-flat building it is the shared line of all the owners.
