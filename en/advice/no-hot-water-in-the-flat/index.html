No hot water in a flat - what the meter in the toilet duct shows

No hot water in a flat in Iļģuciems or elsewhere on Riga’s west side? Open the hot tap and watch the hot water meter. If the dial turns but the water is cold, the water reaches you and is only not heated in the building. If the dial stands still, the water does not reach the meter or stops behind it. First ask the neighbours on the same riser or your building manager whether the riser has been shut off; if they have water, the fault is at the flat’s valve, a filter or the mixer tap.

Four checks with the meter and the taps

  1. The dial turns, the water is cold In series blocks the hot water meter is usually in the boxed-in riser duct in the toilet, next to the riser. If the dial turns, water is flowing through the flat but is not heated. There is nothing to repair in the flat here: report it to your building manager.
  2. The dial stands still, the tap is open The water does not reach the meter or stops right behind it. First find out whether the building manager has shut off the riser because of a repair or a leak: ask the neighbours on the same riser. If they have hot water, look at the flat’s valve: after a repair it can be left half-closed, and on an old valve the handle often turns freely while the inside stays shut.
  3. The dial turns slowly, only one tap is weak The fault is in that tap or its supply. In such a case the manufacturer hansgrohe advises cleaning the filters in front of the mixer tap and in the shower hose seal. Neoperl states that limescale builds up over time in the aerator at the end of the tap and reduces the flow.
  4. Hot at the sink, cold in the thermostatic shower If the sink has hot water but the thermostatic shower mixer gives only cold, the problem is in the mixer’s operating conditions, not in the building. They are described below.

The meter turns: the water is not heated in the building

In many of Riga’s apartment buildings the hot water is heated in the building’s heat substation with district heat. When Rīgas siltums (Riga’s district heating company) tests the networks of the Imanta and Zasulauks heating plants in summer, there is no hot water in the residential areas of Iļģuciems and Grīva either. How to prepare a water heater for that day is written on the page water heaters in Iļģuciems.

If no test has been announced, the dial turns and the water is still cold, the fault is in the building’s system. Under Article 9 of the Residential Building Management Law, the building’s owner is responsible for managing it, which in an apartment building is formed by the flat owners, but in practice the building manager does it. Tell the manager that the meter is turning and the water is cold: that shows at once that the water does arrive at the flat.

Under clause 23.3 of Cabinet Regulation No. 876, a heat user once a year, after agreeing with the supplier, carries out a general inspection and hydraulic tests of the heat substation, heating and hot water systems. So a hot water outage can also be the building’s own test. Checked 16.11.2026.

What the rules say about a flat’s water meter

If hot water comes but the dial does not move, the meter itself may have stopped, and then it no longer tells you anything about the fault. That is a meter problem, not a hot water problem.

The LV portāls (Latvia’s official legal information portal), explaining the position of the Consumer Rights Protection Centre (PTAC, 2026), states that since June 2025 flat water meters no longer have to be verified periodically. The requirement remains for a building’s commercial metering at the incoming supply pipe. Under clause 6.2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 524, in a building with a unified water metering system the flat meters are replaced after the service life set by the manufacturer or, if none is set, no later than 10 years after installation. The flat owners' association decides on introducing such a system by 01.01.2027, taking into account the building manager’s assessment of remotely read meters (clause 34). Checked 16.11.2026.

What the arrangement is in your building, the building manager knows. If a meter is being changed, ask whether it is worth replacing the old valve before or after it at the same time.

A thermostatic shower mixer and its conditions

A thermostatic mixer mixes cold and hot water by itself to the set temperature. The manufacturer Grohe states that all its thermostats are calibrated at the factory at a flow pressure of 3 bar on both sides, and if the temperature differs on site, the thermostat must be readjusted.

  • The hot water at the supply must be at least 2 °C warmer than the desired shower temperature.
  • The flow must be at least 5 l/⁠min; if the static pressure exceeds 5 bar, a pressure reducer is fitted.
  • A thermostat does not work with an unpressurised (open-vented) water heater, and with an instantaneous heater only from 18 kW of power.

So lukewarm hot water from the building or a clogged strainer in the supply can mean that the water at the sink feels warm but the thermostat gives only cold. These figures are Grohe’s requirements; for another manufacturer’s mixer, see its instructions.

A backup water heater: how many hours to hot water

If there is an outage notice in the stairwell, a backup water heater needs time. The manufacturer Gorenje states that with a 2000 W heating element, water heats from 10 °C to 65 °C in 1 h 38 min in a 50-litre tank, 2 h 37 min in an 80-litre tank and 3 h 16 min in a 100-litre tank. If the water heater is on all year, Gorenje advises the “eco” position: the water is kept at 55 °C, and limescale deposits and heat losses are smaller.

A small open-vented water heater under the sink serves one tap and is not suitable for a thermostatic shower. For choosing and connecting a water heater, read water heater installation, and if the backup water heater’s water smells, see water heater smells.

When the dial stands still only in your flat: a job for a plumber

  • A flat valve that no longer opens.
  • A clogged filter in the flat’s supply.
  • A mixer tap or thermostatic mixer that does not let hot water through.
  • Installing or replacing a backup water heater.

Replacing a mixer tap is from €25 for 1 unit, hanging and connecting a water heater from €70 for 1 unit, the plumber’s call-out and inspection from €40. Work on a valve or filter is charged by the hourly rate from €25/h. If a valve before the meter has to be changed, the building manager shuts off the riser. Labour only; materials are extra; whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work.

Questions residents ask

The hot water meter turns, but the water is cold. Am I paying for cold water?

The meter counts the water that passes through the hot water pipe, regardless of its temperature. How this is billed in your building, ask your building manager, and tell them how long the water was cold.

Does a flat’s hot water meter still have to be verified?

The LV portāls, citing PTAC, writes that since June 2025 flat water meters no longer have to be verified periodically. In buildings with a unified metering system they are replaced after the manufacturer’s service life or no later than after 10 years (Cabinet Regulation No. 524).

Can a thermostatic shower mixer be connected to a small water heater under the sink?

If it is an unpressurised (open-vented) water heater, no: Grohe states that thermostats do not work with such heaters. You need a pressurised water heater connected to the water supply with a safety valve.
