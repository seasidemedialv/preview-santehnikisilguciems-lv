If brown water or weak pressure is throughout the building, it is usually the street network. The total length of Rīgas ūdens networks is 1,515 km with an average age of 46.5 years, and the sections in the worst condition are mostly those built in the Soviet period.

In a flat your pipes start at the riser, in a private house at the chamber wall

Under Article 3 of the Apartment Ownership Law, a flat owns the pipes up to the building’s common risers and also the sanitary equipment: toilet, shower, bath. Article 11 of the same law allows construction work in a flat without other owners' consent, as long as it does not affect common property and other flats. So the pipes after the flat’s valves can be replaced by closing only your own valves. The valves at the meters themselves are replaced when the building manager shuts off the riser.

In a private house the owner is responsible for the system from the ownership boundary to the building and inside it. Rīgas ūdens states that the meter chamber at the boundary is built by the property owner. In north-west Imanta, after 10.3 km of new water main was built, 185 households could connect: in each such house the incoming supply pipe from the chamber to the house is the owner’s pipe.

Pipes hum or bang when you close a tap

The manufacturer Caleffi explains that water hammer arises when the flow of water stops suddenly: a single-lever mixer tap closes, a washing machine’s solenoid valve closes, or a ball valve. In Caleffi’s example, in a 10 m long 1/2-inch pipe at a flow of 2 m/⁠s, a sudden closing creates an overpressure of about 28.5 bar in a copper pipe and 18 bar in a PE-X pipe.

In plastic the overpressure is lower, but the manufacturer notes that it yields to the shock more easily, so taps are closed more slowly, and the arrester is placed as close as possible to the fixture that stops the flow. Banging is also made worse by high pressure. LBN 221-15 sets a limit: at the lowest water outlet the pressure must not exceed 0.6 MPa, that is 6 bar; in a tall building, for example a nine-storey series 602 block, the highest pressure is on the lower floors.

Pipes sweat or the cold water in the duct warms up

Sweating is condensation: the air of a warm room cools on a cold pipe. In the toilet’s boxed-in duct the cold pipe runs beside the hot pipe and the towel rail supply, and then it warms up. The manufacturer TECE warns that when warm pipes are laid beside cold drinking water pipes, the cold water must not warm above 25 °C. A new line is therefore run with a gap or with insulation.

Pipe installation and the pressure test

The manufacturer Rehau gives its PE-Xa pipes a maximum working pressure of 10 bar at 70 °C and a short-term emergency temperature of 95 °C, and a slide-on sleeve joint is made without soldering or an open flame. The TECE multilayer system meets class 2 of ISO 10508 and 10 bar, and it must never exceed 100 °C. Rīgas siltums states that a modernised heat substation gives hot water at 50–55 °C, well below these limits.

TECE requires a pressure test to DIN EN 806-4 while all the joints are still visible, and tightness is not judged only by the pressure gauge curve: the joints are also inspected by eye. The manufacturer Viega states that for its press fittings an unpressed joint visibly leaks in a water test at 0.1–0.65 MPa. So pipes are closed into the wall only after the test.

What not to do yourself with new pipes

  • Do not tighten plastic fittings with a pipe wrench: TECE recommends a spanner so that the fitting is not deformed.
  • Do not smear fittings with assembly foam, paint or solvents: TECE does not recommend this for its PPSU fittings.
  • Do not wind too much flax fibre on a thread: TECE warns that it can damage the thread, and after tightening the thread ends must remain visible.
  • Do not store pipes outdoors or on a balcony: UV radiation damages them over time.

What the pipe work price depends on

Laying a pipe along a wall or floor costs from €10/m; concealed laying in a wall costs more. One connection point, for example for a sink, is from €35, fixing a leak from €40, and small jobs at the hourly rate from €25/h. The price changes with the metres, the number of joints, whether the riser shut-off has to be waited for and whether a private house needs digging.

Example: 6 m of new pipe from the duct to the bath and the sink and two connection points come to 6 × €10/m plus 2 × €35, at least €130 in total. Whether the trip is charged separately, the plumber tells you on the call before the work. Labour only; materials are extra.

Questions residents ask

The street was dug up, and now brown water comes from the tap. Do I need to replace the pipes?

Usually not. After work on the street network the water can be cloudy for a while. Let it run until it turns clear, and flush the tap’s aerator. If the weak stream stays only in your flat, then your pipes are checked.

What do PE-Xa, PE-Xb and PE-Xc on a pipe mean?

It is the cross-linking method. The trade magazine Plumber Magazine states that PEX-a is about 85 % cross-linked, PEX-b about 65–70 % and PEX-c about 70–75 %. A kink in PEX-a can be corrected with a hair dryer, but a kinked PEX-b or PEX-c section is cut out and a coupling fitted.

The street has just got a water main. What must a private house build itself?

Rīgas ūdens states that the meter chamber at the ownership boundary is built by the property owner, and from the boundary to the house the pipe is yours. Laying pipe costs from €10/m; digging and the chamber are priced after inspection.

How are pipes tested that will stay empty after the test?

TECE recommends a test with compressed air or an inert gas if the system will be left without water after the test and the water cannot be exchanged within 72 hours at the latest. For hygiene reasons the manufacturer rates this above a test with water.
